## 2. PCS Inverter Capability & PQ Clamping Formulations

### 2.1 Apparent Power Capability Circle Constraint
The PCS inverter operates within a physical apparent power capability boundary defined by maximum IGBT current and thermal dissipation capacity ($S_{\text{max}}$):

$$P^2 + Q^2 \le S_{\text{max}}^2$$

When requested setpoints $(P_{\text{req}}, Q_{\text{req}})$ exceed $S_{\text{max}}$, PowerTwin enforces **Active Power Priority**:

1. Clamp Active Power $P$:

$$P_{\text{accepted}} = \text{clamp}(P_{\text{req}}, -P_{\text{rated}}, +P_{\text{rated}})$$

2. Calculate maximum available Reactive Power headroom:

$$Q_{\text{max\_avail}} = \sqrt{\max(0, S_{\text{max\_effective}}^2 - P_{\text{accepted}}^2)}$$

3. Clamp Reactive Power $Q$:

$$Q_{\text{accepted}} = \text{clamp}(Q_{\text{req}}, -Q_{\text{max\_avail}}, +Q_{\text{max\_avail}})$$

---

### 2.2 Active Power Ramp-Rate Limiter
To prevent rapid power steps that stress electrical grid infrastructure, active power transitions between simulation ticks are bounded by a configurable ramp limit ($\text{Ramp}_{\text{max}}$, e.g. $10\text{ MW/s}$):

$$\Delta P_{\text{max}} = \text{Ramp}_{\text{max}} \cdot \Delta t_{\text{s}}$$

$$P_{\text{actual}}(t) = \text{clamp}\Big(P_{\text{accepted}}(t), \; P_{\text{actual}}(t-1) - \Delta P_{\text{max}}, \; P_{\text{actual}}(t-1) + \Delta P_{\text{max}}\Big)$$

---

### 2.3 Distinction Between Four P & Q Values

PowerTwin explicitly distinguishes between four distinct setpoint and measurement states for audibility and controller-in-the-loop verification:

1. **Requested $P, Q$**: Inbound dispatch target requested by PPC or EMS.
2. **Accepted $P, Q$**: Validated setpoint accepted by PCS after applying PQ capability circle clipping and DC voltage window bounds.
3. **Actual $P, Q$**: Physical AC power output generated by the inverter after applying ramp rate limiters and conversion efficiency losses.
4. **Measured $P, Q$**: Telemetry value reported by simulated AC sensors.

## 3. Difference Between Functional PCS Model and Switching EMT Model

| Attribute | WP04B Functional PCS Model | Detailed Switching EMT Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mathematical Domain** | Steady-state fundamental frequency RMS phasors ($60\text{ Hz}$). | High-frequency electromagnetic transient differential equations ($1 - 100\text{ kHz}$). |
| **Switching Simulation** | Average energy conversion efficiency curve ($\eta = 98.5\%$). | Microsecond pulse-width modulation (PWM) IGBT gate switching dynamics. |
| **Harmonics & Distortion** | Pure fundamental RMS sinusoidal voltage/current. | Total Harmonic Distortion (THD), switching ripple, and filter resonance. |
| **Execution Performance** | Fast execution suitable for $100\text{ Hz}$ real-time digital twin & EMS simulation. | Heavy computation requiring hard real-time FPGA or specialized real-time simulators (OPAL-RT / RTDS). |

## 4. Transformer Loss Formulation

Step-Up Transformer losses are modeled using configurable core (no-load) and copper (load-dependent) loss fractions:

$$S_{\text{actual}} = \sqrt{P_{\text{pri}}^2 + Q_{\text{pri}}^2}$$

$$k = \frac{S_{\text{actual}}}{S_{\text{xfrm\_rated}}}$$

$$P_{\text{loss\_w}} = P_{\text{core\_loss\_w}} + P_{\text{copper\_loss\_w}} \cdot k^2$$

$$P_{\text{sec}} = P_{\text{pri}} - P_{\text{loss\_w}}$$

---

## 5. Switchgear Breaker Interlocks

The MV Switchgear model manages circuit breaker state transitions (`OPEN`, `CLOSED`, `TRIPPED`, `FAULT`). Breaker close commands are strictly rejected if:
1. Physical interlocks are unsafe (`interlock_ok = False`).
2. Over-current trip conditions ($I > 150\% \cdot I_{\text{rated}}$) or active faults are unresolved.
